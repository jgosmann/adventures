After a cold period, it is suddenly summer and hot enough to seek out
north-facing walls. Thus, Elena and I climb the “Bergrettungstour Kufstein”
(“mountain rescue tour Kufstein”) which I have done already
[about two years ago](/posts/240714-bergrettungstour-kufstein/). This time, we
don't get lost because I know the approach from last time. Where it branches off
the road, we pick up another party, staring a bit lost at their smart phones to
find the trail.

<Loc latlong={[47.59796, 12.20204]} />

Elena starts with the first pitch. This means I get to lead all the pitches that
I was following last time. The start is still a bit tricky, but seems easier
than I remember it.

<Rimg src="1.jpeg" alt="Bergrettungstour Kufstein" overlay="1.svg" />

In the third pitch, Elena has to use aid to get over the crux of the pitch
because she couldn't reach a hold. At first, I can't reach it either when
following her, but then use a toe hook with my left foot behind a flake,
allowing me to lean far enough over to the right. I don't remember how I solved
it last time.

<Rimg src="2.jpeg" caption="The fifth pitch." />
<Rimg src="3.jpeg" alt="Bergrettungstour Kufstein" />

I get to lead the crux pitch. It is very well protected with three close bolts.
I skip one of them because in the moment it seemed safer to continue to the
third bolt, where I have good holds to clip, rather than wasting energy trying
to clip the second bolt in a bad position.

<Pano2x src="1.jpeg" alt="View from the Bergrettungstour Kufstein" />

The chimney in the ninth pitch that gave me trouble last time is surprisingly
completely dry. I still find it somewhat hard and uncomfortable, even though I'm
following this time. My foot also slips in one spot. Elena used her panic draw
to make it through leading.

Afterwards, some more enjoyable climbing and some layback cracks await us, before
topping out. On these last few pitches, we are in the sun, but there is some wind
making it bearable. It was a quite enjoyable climb with completely dry rock,
perfect temperatures for the most part, and a pretty consistent difficulty.

After a late lunch break (it's already half past 4!) at the
top, we descend back to Kufstein. As we used the train to get to Kufstein, and
we don't have to get back to a parked car at the start of the route, we decide
to take a different trail than the usual descent, on which we are longer on
a hiking trail than walking through the city. The map on my smartphone did show
a lot of switch backs down to a river. So we expected a steep descent. But we
didn't expect to end up in a beautiful gorge with a steep ascent on the other
side. As it consists of a steal stair case, it didn't show up as switch backs.
But all in all it is a nice trail back to Kufstein.
