This night I slept much better. We skip yoga today and after breakfast we meet
with our guide for a self-rescue workshop. We walk to the Thorstein, where we
learn how to escape a belay and how to haul up another climber.

<Loc latlong={[49.604185, 11.643072]} zoom={15} />

After three hours, the workshop ends, we have a lunch break, and stay right
there to do some of the climbs. Some offer good opportunities to practice gear
placement a little bit. Some other climbers are trying Königswampe, a <Grade
system="UIAA" value="7-" />, and apparently find it hard and a bit scary. After
they are finished, I get on it, and manage to onsight it.

<Rimg src="1.jpeg" caption="Thorstein" />

We finish the day with Eddie, another <Grade system="UIAA" value="7-" />, but it
initially feels much harder. It takes a little bit to figure out the moves, but
I can redpoint it in my second try.

We then walk back to the festival grounds, and get some food for dinner. While
standing in line for crêpes as dessert, we can watch the part of the deep water
solo contest. The festival is on the grounds of an open-air swimming pool. They
put up a bouldering wall at the pool side and have a contest who can climb it
fastest. Unfortunately, it starts to rain again while we still stand in line.
This wasn't expected and I don't have my rain jacket with me. I flee into the
washrooms to wait out the rain shower.

It stops raining, I go back to the tent to retrieve my rain jacket, wait out
another rain shower in the tent, before going back to the festival stage to
watch an entertaining talk by [Alex
Megos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Megos), who is from the
Franconion Jura. The talk is followed by the reggae music act Jamaram. The music
act is followed by an actual thunderstorm. Which makes us and many others flee
to their tents and vans. It passes quickly and there would be more music acts
(that can be heard well in the tent despite earplugs), but I stay in the tent
and prefer to try to sleep. Despite the music, I even somewhat succeed.
