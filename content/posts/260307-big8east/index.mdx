With the beginning of March, we also got nice, sunny spring weather. This
Saturday, I use it for a gravel ride to try out a route posted by my bicycle
shop. It loops in the form of an eight in the East of Munich.

<GpxTrack src="20260307.gpx" />

After a first stretch through the city, I enter gravel paths. For most of the
day, the route goes through forests on gravel with only short stretches being on
asphalt. And there are all variation of gravel from compact and hard over
loose and fine to bumpy pebbles. In the beginning it is fun to ride. Two or
three times, at very rough patches, I even make use of my dropper seat post and
feel like it is actually useful and not just a gimmick. Nevertheless, hitting
some asphalt after a gravel stretch also always feels nice because the riding is
suddenly so much easier.

About a quarter in, the road suddenly ends and I don't see the path that should
exist according to my GPX track. While there is no proper gravel road, there is
a straight lane without trees through the forest. I guess, that must be the way.
First, I have to get over the remains of some trees and then ride a bumpy,
grassy path. At least I feel like my bicycle, with its big tyres and low
gearing, is made for this.

Not all hardships have been overcome, yet. As I'm entering the outer loop of the
eight, the landscape gets more hilly with some steep inclines, but also speedy
descents. I encounter also some (very short) muddy stretches and more debris of
trees that I had to carry my bicycle over. At one particular hard incline my
chain came off even though this shouldn't happen as easily with a narrow wide
chain ring.

At about the point farthest away from home, I follow a little bit some paths
that I have been on at a [hike in the Mangfall valley a few years
back](/posts/220415-mangfalltal).

After completing the outer loop, I miss a turn and end up joining back onto the
inner loop a few kilometers later than intended. I now have to cycle these few
kilometers once more on the inner loop.

At about 90km I feel that it is actually enough for the day. Riding
bumpy gravel all day is quite tiring. Not just for my legs, but also the arms
that need to cushion the bumps and get shaken a lot on the rough surfaces.
Daylight will also get less soon and I've eaten all my food. Anyways, I fight
it out and after 132km and 7 hours of riding time I am back home. This gives an
average speed 18.7km/h. I reached a maximum speed of 58.4km/h.
