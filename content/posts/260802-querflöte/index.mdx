After breakfast, we walk again to the Guggerköpfele, but a little bit farther.
There we start the route Querflöte in teams split by gender. The first two
pitches are good warm ups for the following <Grade system="UIAA" value="7+" />
crux pitch. No one of use climbs it clean, but it is quite cool climbing. First
you have to downclimb a few moves, before you follow sort of a crack. First on
sidepulls and pockets going up and then turning into a right-ward traverse on
underclings. All with quite the drop below you. Also the next pitch is quite
engaging climbing.

When we are finished, it is early afternoon. As mid-afternoon thunderstorms
might occur, we decide that it is time to descent back to the cabin. Indeed,
shortly after we are back, it starts to rain. We pass the time until dinner with
showering and playing my climbing card came
[“5.15”](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/313766/515-a-rock-climbing-card-game).

A while after dinner, some are already sleeping in their bunk beds, two young
women complain that Marco and I are occupying their bunks, that the only
remaining bunks are not next to each other, and that they paid to have beds next
to each other. We were told it is a free choice of bunks despite assigned
numbers given (our numbers were also taken on our arrival). We also think its
a first come, first served, and there is no guarantee for beds next to each
other. In the end, another woman, travelling alone, agrees to move, so that two
bunks next to each other are free. Definitely the most rational solution to have
only one person move than two persons staying for several nights.
