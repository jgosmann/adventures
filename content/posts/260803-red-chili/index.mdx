Once again, with unstable weather conditions in the afternoon, we decide for
shorter multipitches at the Guggerköpfle. Sarah and Marco do Eiertanz <Grade
system="UIAA" value="7-" />. Anna and I start on Traumtänzer. The first pitch is
graded <Grade system="UIAA" value="7" /> and the first part is fine to climb.
But the end is quite hard for the grade. At the anchor we look at the crux pitch
graded <Grade system="UIAA" value="8-" />. It is a short traverse under a roof.
We could probably get somehow through, but are not super motivated. Also some of
the bolts are blocked with slings, i.e. you can only clip into the sling, but
not the bolt directly, or you would have to remove the slings. The slings don’t
look particularly new and thus it is unclear whether they can still be trusted.
In the end, we decide to rappel back down.

<Loc latlong={[47.265624, 10.669932]} zoom={16} />

We then do Red Chili <Grade system="UIAA" value="7-" /> instead. The three
pitches are pretty consistent in difficulty and feature some good climbing on
pockets. Only in the second pitch (the one I’m following on), I don’t see the
sequence (which was probably there) and end up sitting in the rope.

After finishing the multi-pitch, we descend back down and climb the single-pitch
On the moon <Grade system="UIAA" value="7-" /> (also quite nice climbing with
pockets). After having cleaned the route, we hear some thunder. A sure sign that
is about time to get back to the cabin. On the way, we collect Sarah and Marco,
who were doing some sport routes a bit father down. When we arrive back at the
cabin, it is already raining lightly, but not enough to get really wet.

We pass the time to dinner again with some games. Also this evening, we
encounter people not really knowledgeable about the mountain cabin etiquette.
When lying already in our bunk beds, shortly before 10pm, two women arrive and
turn the light back on, even noticing that some are already sleeping, but
keeping the light on anyways until told to turn it off.
