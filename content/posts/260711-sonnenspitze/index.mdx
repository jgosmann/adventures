For years, I wanted to climb the Ehrwalder Sonnenspitze (2416m).
It never worked out. [The one attempt in 2024](/posts/240629-bernadeinkopf/) was
already spoiled by the unreliability of German trains. But by now there is
a direct train connection to Ehrwald, which simplifies things.

I take my bicycle with me because the train station is a bit far from the hiking
trails (though there are bus connections), and it will save me some time. On the
way, I get already a good view of the Sonnenspitze.

<Rimg src="1.jpeg" caption="The Sonnenspitze." />

The direct hiking trail is too steep to use a bicycle. Thus, I have to take
a longer way around. Most of the steep ascent is paved (but closed to cars)
which is nice, but my leg muscles are complaining more than I expected, assuming
that I'm still in training from [my bicycle tour with lots of
elevation](/posts/260616-bernau/). Nevertheless, I manage and then continue on
a wide gravel path looping around to the Seeebenalm. There are a lot of hikers
here. A little bit above the Seeebenalm is the Seeebensee (Seeeben lake), where
I leave my bicycle. Getting here took me about 90 minutes.

<Pano2x src="2.jpeg" caption="The Miemiger Ketter on the way up." />

<Rimg src="2.jpeg" caption="Seeebensee in front of the Sonnenspitze." />

From here, I ascend to the Coburger Hütte (cabin) with a lot of other people.
After that, it gets a bit quieter going westward towards the Biberwiererscharte.
But before reaching it, I turn onto a screefield to ascend towards the
Sonnenspitze. There are already a few other people in the scree field that help
a bit to find the right direction.

After a short while, the scree ends and it gets more fun, walking and climbing
on actual somewhat solid rock. It isn't particularly difficult. The crux in
a chimney is graded <Grade system="UIAA" value="2" />, but doesn't feel very
distinct and doesn't pose a problem. There I overtake two other pairs of
climbers. Now I am in front and need to find the route. As the crux is followed
by a short steel rope, it is easy for that bit. In most other parts, I also have
no trouble finding the route by following the traces of polished rock,
occasional anchors, and markings. Only for a few meters I end up besides the
actual route. I was aware that I'm not quite on the route early on there, but
the climbing was also not really any harder, so I stuck with it until I joined
back to the proper route a few meters higher up.

Then the terrain gets a bit flatter, even walking terrain, but has a short quite
narrow stretch with a steep drop off. I'm unfazed, however. From the first
summit, it goes over a narrow ridge line with another climbing spot, before one
is on the main summit, where I have a lunch break.

To descend, I take the north-west ridge line. There are two more spots that need
to be down climbed, but apart from that it is more like a difficult hiking
trail. In many parts, it has quite a bit loose gravel on it, which is annoying as
it can one make slip easily.

At the Seeebensee, I change back to my bicycle. At the Seeebenalm, I have another
stop to get something to drink and a bite to eat. Then I ride back to the train
station. I have a few short, not so steep climbs, before it is just rolling
down, and in about 30 to 40 minutes, I'm back at train station.

I was moving for 5:15h and covered a distance of 35.8km.
