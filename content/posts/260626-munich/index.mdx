It's the last vacation day. After another great breakfast, I start by going north
and then west around the Wilde Kaiser and Zahme Kaiser. Here I get one more
steep ascent of 150m. There is a street sign stating an incline of 15%, but it
doesn't feel any steeper than the Großglockner high alpine road which has an
incline of at most 12% and 9% around average.

A bit more up and down follows, as I pass in-between the mountains westwards.
For a while, I follow a nice flowy gravel trail. Eventually, I hit the Inn and
follow it north. It is starting to get quite warm now, the temperature may climb
above 30°C today. Along the inn aren't any trees providing shade. But eventually
I come across a reconstructed boat and some informative displays about life
along the Inn, which happens to be covered by roof providing shade for an
overdue (early) lunch break at 11am. I already did 40km.

I follow the Inn farther north and leave the mountains behind me. Shortly before
Rosenheim, I turn westward and now follow the Mangfall. In Brückmühl, after
about 80km, I have a second (late) lunch break at 2pm. I get an isotonic drink,
potato salad, and some fish with it. As desert I get an ice cream.

Soon I leave the Mangfall, the mountains are getting quite small in the distance
already, and head towards Aying. In the beer garden of the local brewery, I hav
a non-alcoholic wheat beer to rehydrate before tackling the last 20-ish
kilometers back to Munich.

<Rimg
  src="15.jpeg"
  caption="The mountains now quite small and far in the distance."
/>

Around 6pm, I arrive at home and my vacation comes to an end. It was a good mix
of type 1 and type 2 fun. It is hard to believe that I actually managed all of
the 13,000m of elevation without major problems. But now I’m also happy not
having to cycle up mountains for a while.

Most of the route was on either car-free trails or low traffic roads, apart from
some short stretches and the Großglockner high alpine road. But my subjective
feeling is that on other official cycling routes the proportion of major roads
is often higher. This is certainly in part also because of putting in the effort
to go up mountain trails. While the route as such wasn't signed specifically,
most of it followed existing cycling trails or mountain bike trails that were
signed, so navigation (in combination with the GPX tracks) did work quite well.

The stats: 125km in 6:30h (w/o breaks), 19.4km/h average, 740m elevation gain.
