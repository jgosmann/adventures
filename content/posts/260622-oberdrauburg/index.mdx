Today, breakfast begins at 7am, so I manage to start shortly after 8am. When
I put on my helmet, I feel something wet running down my forehead, which is
weird. I take off my helmet again and I am surprised by an earthworm crawling
inside it. It must have gotten in there when I put the helmet down on the
ground a few minutes earlier.

The route is fairly flat for a bit, before it slowly gets steeper. But it won't
get as steep as the previous days. I pass by the remains of the castle Edenvest,
the first castle in the region to be named in a document. There isn't much left,
but the steep and narrow hiking trail onto the rock is quite nice and a bit
mystical.

Back on my bike, the ascent continues along another nice mountain
stream. It is quite long, I have to get back up to about 1700m of elevation.
Eventually, it flattens out and I cycle through a beautiful plateau. Then, a long
descent Krems and farther along Gmünd follows. It feels like it's not going to
end at all. In Gmünd, I have lunch, Kärntner Nudeln, a local speciality, somewhat
similar to dumplings with different fillings (spinach, cheese, mushrooms).

Unfortunately, I haven't even covered half of the distance for the day. I now
follow the Drau cycling route, which is flatter, but still has some up and
downs. It seems to be quite popular as I'm seeing masses of cyclists in certain
parts.

Down in the valley it is super hot. Sometimes, I have a bit of head winds, but
often these feel like a hair dryer. Only towards the end of today's route, some
cloud cover appears. There is even a little rain shower right when I wanted to
do a short break anyways. Perfect timing, and under some trees, I'm not even
getting wet.

A bit more riding and I finally make it to Oberdrauburg. Given that I was hoping
for a more relaxed day after yesterday, I’m again quite tired. After getting
comfortable in a roomy hotel room and showering, I have smoked trout and apple
strudel for dinner. After a walk around the city core (I discover a small
climbing crag), I relax in my hotel room.
