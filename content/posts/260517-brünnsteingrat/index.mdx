The weather forecast for today promised a somewhat sunny, but cold day,
in-between more rainy days. Thus, I decided for a bike and hike tour in in the
Bavarian foothills of the Alps onto the Brünnstein (1634m), where the elevation
is a bit lower and potential coldness and snow won't be as much of a problem. Also,
I need to train for an upcoming cycling tour with more than 13,000m of
elevation.

Around 9am, I start in Oberaudorf, and it doesn't take long before the first 16%
incline. At least on the way up I will be warm, even sweating a lot. So far the
road is still paved, but soon the gravel road begins. I make my way slowly up
the mountain. Eventually I get to a fork in the road and I could take either
way. I decide for the left variant, but after another 150m of elevation
I realize that this wasn't a good choice (though, I couldn't have known). Every
couple of meters they dug a ravine across the path with high sidewalls of loose
dirt. Together with the steep incline, there is just no way for me to ride over
these. Thus, I end up pushing my bike until it gets flatter.

When the alternate paths join back together, these obstacles stop. But now its
getting extremely steep with inclines above 20%. I give it a few tries, but
eventually have to accept that I still have to push my bike. On the gravel,
I need to bring enough weight on the back wheel, so that it doesn't slip (which
the geometry of my bike already supports well). But
then, with the center of mass so far back and the low gearing, the front wheel
tends to lift up and I lose control. If I put more weight onto the front of my
bike, my back wheel starts slipping and I also lose control.

Eventually, I make it over the steepest parts and can mount my bicycle again.
Unfortunately, it starts raining small ice pellets. This wasn't expected! At
first it is not much, but then I'm being convinced to get out my rain jacket.
I'm not far from the Brünnsteinhaus (1360m), a mountain cabin. So I make my way
there, lock my bicycle, and consider what to do next.

First, I get a hot tea and a piece of cake at the cabin. The weather radar
reveals that I apparently chose pretty much the only spot in the wider area
where there is precipitation. At least the chance of precipitation should
decrease soon and indeed it has stopped by the time I'm finished with the cake
and tea.

Thus, I continue with my ascent to the Brünnstein on foot along the Dr. Julius
Mayr via ferrata (A/B). Not long after, the ice pellets start again, but the rock
stays mostly dry. I make it to the summit without any problems and by that time
the ice pellets have stopped. It is also not particularly cold and I even put
away my gloves. On the summit is a small chapel.

As the weather is back to being dry, I continue with the Brünnsteingrat (the
ridge line). There is no official trail here and it involves some very easy
climbing of at most <Grade system="UIAA" value="1" />. In parts it is quite
narrow and the Northern side drops off sharply. In some parts I have to descend
a bit to the South under the ridge line where there is still (or again) a thin
snow cover. There are no tracks in it, so I'm probably the first person today to
come along here. Via the Paulskopf (1633m), I get to the Peterskopf (1634m),
where I take in the view.

Then I retrace my steps back to the Brünnstein and descend it via the normal
hiking trail back to Brünnsteinhaus. There I have a Kaspressknödelsuppe. When
checking for the trains back, the next one departs in 30 minutes, and then it is
a 60 minute wait. I estimate that I will probably only need about 30 minutes to
get to the train station, thanks to having invested in bringing a bike up here.
As I don't want to kill another hour, I decide to try it.

It is fun to roll down the mountain, even though I have to be careful and go
slow in the steepest parts. As it gets flatter and I get into Oberaudorf, it is
a race against the time. Now I have to pedal again. At the train station, I have
to carry my bike down some stairs and back up to get to the right platform. Just
as I arrive at the platform at 2:10pm, the train also arrives. Great timing!

Usually, this would have been the end of it. Unfortunately, the train ends in
Grafing because some children are running around in the train tracks ahead.
Instead you are supposed to take the S-Bahn, but of course there isn't one going
towards Munich for the same reason. Instead, one would have to go the opposite
direction to the terminal stop, change there to a different S-Bahn and later
change to the subway. All in all it would at least take another our. Given that
I have my bicycle with me, I decide to make the best out of it, and cycle the
remaining 34km to Munich. I pass through some nice forests, and trough
beautiful yellow rapeseed fields in full bloom exuding a nice smell.
