Today, Elena and I are tagging along with Christina and Josef, who offered a ride
to the Guffert (2195m, [I've been on it previously](/posts/190831-guffert/)).
There are a few choices of multi-pitch routes and we make the final decision
during the approach. As Elena is worried about cold fingers, we forgo the harder
option Da Capo <Grade system="UIAA" value="7-" /> and decide like Christina and
Josef for the South ridge line (Südgrat) <Grade system="UIAA" value="5+" />. The
first ascensionists were the famous Hans Fiechtl and [Hans
Dülfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_D%C3%BClfer) in 1911. Thus, it is
a quite old route!

<Loc latlong={[47.54426, 11.78822]} />

The last part of the approach we scramble up runlets on the slab. Elena is in
her happy place here. At the start of the route, we find one party before us and
another party rappelling as they decided to retreat. They are a bit worried
about a potential thunderstorm. We check the weather again and we should still
be fine until the evening, so we prepare ourselves for the route.

I get to lead the first pitch <Grade system="UIAA" value="5-" />. It starts
tricky and it takes me a while to get off the ground. Even before that,
I already place a friend to feel a bit safer, and shortly after that a second
one. It is also quite polished, but this is no surprise to us for such an old
route and because it is mentioned in pretty much very description. After the
first few moves the pitch gets easier.

Elena gets to lead the second pitch <Grade system="UIAA" value="5-" /> where she
also places a few friends in addition to the few bolts. The pitch follows a nice
flake, but is also quite polished. It is followed by mostly walkable, but in one
spot exposed, terrain leading to the crux pitch.

Here we have to wait for the party of four before us. Luckily, next to the
start of the pitch is a grassy platform where we can have a relaxed lunch break.
There, we also switch around the rope ends because I get to lead again.
Not far into the pitch, the crux <Grade system="UIAA" value="5+" /> awaits me. It is
a slab with not much to hold onto, right above a ledge. It soon gets
flatter and easier, before reaching the next bolt, but first I have to get there.
I attempt to climb up a few times, but each time downclimb back to the ledge,
afraid to commit. But each time I understand a little bit better where useable
holds and feet are. Eventually, I find a sequence a bit farther left and can make
it over the crux. The first ascensionists wrote about this part: “Short, but
extremely difficult and dangerous traverse across the smooth slab.” I'm happy
that I don't have to do it with 1911's equipment.

But the pitch isn't finished yet. A steeper (and of course polished) part
follows. While not as precarious, still demanding. Elena and Christina are a bit
impressed that I was able to lead this. It seems that I'm not as affected by
polished rock because they are complaining way more about it.

To reach the final official pitch, led by Elena, we have to walk again a bit.
Then the remainder towards the summit can also be walked, but there is also an
option to have some more easy climbing that we decide for. Too lazy to switch
shoes once more, we just climb it in our approach shoes.

<Pano2x src="1.jpeg" caption="Just under the summit." />

At the summit, Elena and I have to wait for Christina and Josef, who are a bit
behind us, and belay also in the easy terrain at the end. By now it got quite
cloudy, windy, and a bit cold. It also has gotten somewhat late, even though we
didn't really waste much time on the route (but we had to wait for the party
before us). So the potential thunderstorm in the evening could now become a real
danger. Thus, we start our descent as soon as Christina and Josef make it to the
summit.

As we get down, the temperature increases and we reach more wind-protected areas.
So we are quickly warm again. We make it to the car without a drop of rain. But
as we arrive in Munich and most of us have to cycle home, we get hit by the
downpour.

Despite the low nominal grade of the route, it was quite demanding and a great
adventure. At least if you're not too much bothered by the polished rock. The
others also agreed that the grading is quite stiff. I'm not sure that this
is purely because of the polished rock; maybe climber's back in the day were
just more hardcore.
