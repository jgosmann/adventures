All night long it was storming, and even now it is still extremely windy. Today,
we drive to the same parking lot as yesterday, but descend on the other side of
the road to the Antenes crag (located below some large TV antennas). We start
again with some easier routes. As my third route, I start to lead Laia <Grade
system="french" value="6a" />, but after the first two bolts, the remaining
bolts turn out to be quite rusty. I’m still a bit shaky mentally (maybe also
from the experience yesterday), and decide I don’t want to risk it. Thus,
I lower off on a carabiner. To retrieve it, I attempt to start Climber Up <Grade
system="french" value="6a+" />. However, the start isn’t so easy and quite
painful (Elena later climbs it). In the end, I climb Esperó de l’Alba <Grade
system="UIAA" value="5" /> from where I can lower off at the top to retrieve my
carabiner.

Then, we drive to the Refugi de la Mussara, a mountain cabin, that will be our
accommodation for the next nights. After checking in — again, only few other
guests are staying there, with only us in a room of 15 bunk beds —, we walk to
the ruins of the La Mussara village and enjoy the sunset before dinner. The
first course is a salad, the second course pasta with tomato sauce. Yoghurt is
for desert. Everything is much more basic here than in the already basic youth
hostel. To shower, we have to go to a building outside of the main building, but
at least there are warm showers (but the building itself isn’t heated).
