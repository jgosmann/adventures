Fabian, Birgit, and I are climbing the Große Waxenstein (2277m) today. We take
the first train in the morning at 5:27am because it is a long tour. We start
hiking before 8am from Untergrainau. First we hike along the Hammersbach stream,
then through the Höllentalklamm, and eventually reach the Höllentalangerhütte (a
mountain cabin). Already this part is always impressive.

Now we leave the official trails and cross a scree field to the West. The start
of our trail is marked by a big red “W”. We already put on our helmets when
leaving the official trail, which turns out to be a good decision because right
when we get close to the wall, two rocks fall from above. Luckily they do not hit
anyone.

The first part of the trail gains some height quickly, and is in parts quite
narrow and exposed. There are also some <Grade system="UIAA" value="1" /> spots
that require the use of hands. Besides not being an official trail, and the
guide book saying that it isn't well marked, it turns out to be quite well
marked. Also without marks it is usually quite recognizable. Thus, we make good
progress to about 2000m of elevation (and don't encounter anymore rockfall).

Now a path to the Zwölferkopf, Mittagsscharte, and Kleine Waxenstein branches
off. Shortly after is another branch leading to the Große Waxenstein. Here the
terrain gets much more rocky and involves climbing up to <Grade system="UIAA"
value="2" />. But it is in a gully and not particularly exposed. The
difficulties are rather that the rock is fragile in spots and even the solid
parts are in parts covered in rubble. Apart from that, the sun doesn't reach
into the gully very well, and there are some old snow fields. In part, they are
helpful because the snow is well packed and one can make good steps in it. But
we also have to be careful not to slip in the steeper parts or to break through
where the snow has molten from below (mainly a problem at the ends of
the snow fields).

On the summit, we have a lunch break and enjoy the great view of the Eibsee Lake
as well as the ridge line from Alpspitze to Zugspitze.

We first descent on the same way that we came up. Going down on the snow
fields, it is a bit more difficult than going up, but we manage. Using two hand
sized rocks (which there are enough of lying around everywhere) works pretty well
as makeshift ice picks. While going down there are surprisingly many other
parties still going up.

Back at the branching point, we follow the Schafsteig to the Riffelkar. This
trail is a bit less well marked, and there are a few spots where we loose the
trail or are unsure where it continues before rediscovering it a bit later. But
being alert we always catch our mistakes before ending up in more difficult
terrain.

Eventually we hit the official hiking trail that brings us back to the
Höllentalangerhütte. After a short water refill, we continue our descent. It is
much more crowded now, and many have not really appropriate foot wear. Though,
my own mountain boots are starting to lose their soles. So I suppose it was the
last tour for them and I will switch to my second pair permanently.

After about 9 hours, excluding our lunch break, we're back in Hammersbach where
we take the Zugspitzbahn back to Garmisch, and then the train back to Munich.
We've ascended about 1675m and descended a bit less.
