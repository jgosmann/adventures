I’m planning to do the ridge line from the Pyramidenspitze (1997m) to the
Roßkaiser (1978m) in the Zahmer Kaiser. Not fully convinced by the bus
connection from (and especially back to Kufstein), I decided to take my bicycle
with me and ride it from Oberaudorf. This will also allow me to cycle the first
part of the ascent. It turns out to be a good decision as my train is delayed by
30 minutes and I would have missed the connecting bus. It runs only once an
hour and would have increased the delay even more.

<GpxTrack src="20260808.gpx" />

When the train arrives, it is jam-packed, but somehow I get lucky and can still
get in with my bicycle. From Oberaudorf, I cycle to the Zahme Kaiser, partially
on the route that I [rode back to Munich on my big cycling tour this
year](/posts/260626-munich/). Unfortunately, my left thigh is complaining quite
a bit, but after a while it gets better. Eventually, after about 500m of
elevation and 1:10h, the gravel road ends and I have to stow away my bicycle.
Now, I’m ascending towards the Pyramidenspitze by foot.

I overtake a few other hikers. The grassy path turns into a slope of gravel
before the rock gets more solid and becomes an easy via ferrata. A good while
in, I arrive at a really large trail marking pointing to the left. But to the
right is also a faint trail. It leads to the Jovenspitze (1892m). Not following
such a prominent trail marking feels rebellious and I follow the faint trail.
I overtake two slightly older men on the trail who ask me whether I have done it
before and if the trail just follows up the ravine. I say my best guess is as
good as theirs. A while later, it is not so obvious anymore where the trail is
(if there is one). I just ascent where the terrain seems easiest to me. It might
not have been the perfect route, but it brings me to my first summit of the day.

Going down, as often, turns out to be harder. There are parts where one has to
be careful not to slip on loose gravel. But from this direction, there is
a visible trail visible (that I wasn’t on, on my way up), so I follow it. There
are even some cairns. But instead of bringing me down, this trail stays much
more at the same elevation and eventually it seemingly ends. As this is
definitely not the way I came up, I’m unsure where the trail continues and all
potential options seem to end in dangerously steep areas. Thus, I decide it is
safest to go back and try to find the way I ascended on. Even this isn’t so easy
without a clear trail. Luckily, I’ve recorded my route on my GPS device which
helps to make it safely back to the official trail. Back at home, I will look it
up and find out that there apparently is a second trail that would have hit the
official trail without loosing as much elevation again. But it is stated to be
at least as difficult as the trail I took and that the trail can only be seen
with a bit of imagination.

On the official trail, I’m now just behind the hikers I overtook earlier. So
I suppose I didn’t loose too much time. Soon after, I’m on the summit of the
Pyramidenspitze.

After a short break, I start following the ridge line. The first few meters
follow an official trail, where I almost miss the point where the trail turns
away from the ridge line and descents. Here I must leave the trail and ascend
trail-less over grassy slopes to the Vordere Keselschneid (2002m), the highest
point in the Zahme Kaiser (many wrongly believe the Pyramidenspitze is the
highest point).

Past this summit, also the south-facing wall gets steeper and the ridge line
becomes narrower, felling more like an actual ridge line. There are also the
first few bits that need to be downclimbed, graded <Grade system="UIAA" value="2"
/>. After a while, the ridge widens again and the ridge splits. I go up to the
south on a grassy slope to the summit of the Hintere Kesselschneid (1995m).

After turning back to the main ridge line, the prime part awaits: crossing over
to the Große Roßkaiser (1978m). From here, on can question whether this is
actually possible. But as I get close to each steep looking spot, there is
a possibility to downclimb (<Grade system="UIAA" value="2" />) or go around. At
times, it is definitely a narrow ridge with steep drop-offs to either side.
A good head for heights is absolutely essential here. It is surprisingly quiet
here. On the complete ridge line, I don’t see anyone else.

From far, the ascent on the west face of the Große Roßkaiser looked quite hard,
but from up close, it is not harder than the other parts. This ends the most
technical part. The short crossing over to the Kleine Roßkaiser (1922m) is
already easier and less exposed. After this sixth summit, I descend on a steep
and narrow, but clearly visible, trail (still unmarked).

I’m supposed to hit and follow an official trail eventually. But this trail
turns out to be much less visible in the steep cow paddock and to be not very
well marked. After descending a bit too much and going up again, I eventually
find it, but loose it again, before finding it again with help of my
GPS. After leaving the cow paddock, it gets quite narrow again, but at least it
is easier to follow now. It is definitely not a trail taken by many. A single
hiker crosses my path. I’m definitely getting quite tired from all the elevation
gain, but also the heat. I was hoping for a wider, easy to walk trail, but now
I have to push through.

At a saddle, I have the option to do another 150m of elevation to summit the
Heuberg (1603m). As a would likely miss the train at 6pm either way, I go up
there on the now easier trail and complete my seventh summit of the day, which
is a nice number.

<Rimg src="26.jpeg" caption="Walchsee as seen from the Heuberg." />

Going down, unfortunately, I get some pain on the side of my right knee. If I’m
careful, I can somewhat avoid it and the not so steep parts are also still fine.
So I manage to descend the remaining 600m of elevation to my bicycle. I’m happy
to have made it back. Now I can just let it roll for a bit before cycling a few
more kilometers or about 40 minutes. My knee is fine with this and with this
different kind of movement, I don’t feel quite as tired. Back in Oberaudorf,
I still have time to get an icecream.

It was definitely a great tour, though the descent and way back got quite tiring
and strenuous. It took me over eight hours and I covered over 2600m of
elevation. This is much more than 1550m stated by the guidebook, but this didn’t
include the Jovenspitze and Heuberg, and started about 400m higher up than
Oberaudorf. Still this doesn’t quite add up because excluding this, I’m still at
300-400m of elevation more.
