For our last climbing day, we decide to visit once more the Antenes crag, but
a different sector (Diedres and del Mig). Again, we start with some easier
routes. In the morning, my mental game isn't quite there, and I down climb
Donantes de Sangre <Grade system="UIAA" value="5+" /> before the first bolt
because I don’t want to commit to a particular move that seems more difficult to
down climb, and it seems to me that after that move, at least another non-trivial
move is necessary to reach the bolt. Elena takes over the lead, and can reach
the bolt without any particularly hard moves, but it is just barely in her
reach. After her onsight, I have more confidence (also with the draw
pre-clipped, I don’t have to reach as high), I manage to flash the route.

Later, we climb Jarto d’Estar Harto <Grade system="french" value="6a+" />, a very
nice route featuring dihedrals, cracks, and some face climbing. My mental game
is improving and next I feel confident enough for Sufre Mamón <Grade
system="french" value="6b" />. It first follows the <Grade system="UIAA"
value="5+" /> Xell, which is quite fun, before adding some more easy nice moves on
blocky terrain that lead into the corner of a roof protruding to the right. This
is the single powerful crux of the route. From an undercling, one has to bump up
the left foot to gain the reach for a big sidepull in the corner of the roof.
Then you layback, get up your foot, hold the tension, reach for some slightly
juggy holds in the corner above the roof, and try to get up your other foot
above the roof. Then, it gets a bit easier again to the top. Due to the length of
thirty meters, you get a great view from the top.

Then, we move on to Espantocells <Grade system="french" value="6a" /> and it’s
extension <Grade system="french" value="6b" /> which are quite enjoyable too.
Supposedly, Pelut <Grade system="french" value="6b" /> is good too. Elena
doesn’t have such a bad time on it, but when I do it after her, I find the holds
at the start quite painful. Also her beta doesn’t work for me (too painful), but
can figure out some other (still somewhat painful) beta. After the start, it is
quite nice face climbing, though.

To end the trip, we want to do Via del Marro <Grade system="UIAA" value="5+" />.
It has two pitches, but according to the guide book, our rope should be long
enough to do it as one long pitch. Elena climbs it first, then I do it. I find
the start extremely hard and powerful and nowhere close to the <Grade
system="UIAA" value="5+" /> grade. Afterwards, it gets much easier and feels too
easy for the grade. It also turns out that the length information in the
guidebook is incorrect. The first pitch is already 35 meters, so we don’t climb
the second pitch that would go up a rock tower and would have given a good
sunset view (though I couldn't see where the bolts continue for that line).

This concludes the climbing part of the trip. We have climbed quite a few nice
routes. Also, the area is everything else but overrun. The first days we were
mostly alone at the crags. Only on the weekend, we met some more people, but it
was hardly overcrowded. Some routes are bolted very well, others have more
runout distances. Unfortunately, on some routes some of the bolts are rusty and
it is unclear how trustworthy they still are. At least one route that we would
have liked to climb, we didn't climb because of this. The grading as a bit on
the stiffer side (as we were expecting and is stated in the guide book), but
occasionally routes with easy grades have single spots that are much harder and
not even close to the assigned grade in my opinion. This doesn't necessarily
help to give confidence that harder routes that should be in my grade range will
actually be climbable and thus I did stay below my max climbing grade in this
vacation. For pushing grades, other areas are more suitable.

On the drive back to the refugi for our last night, we see a dark fox crossing
our path. For dinner we get a giant portion of rice with tomato sauce, topped with
a fried egg. As if this wasn't enough, the second course is another decent load
of beans and those antipasti vegetable from the day before. It is the first day,
where I don’t finish and leave some beans. The desert choices didn't grow this
time, but we only get offered yoghurt or flan (we decide for the latter).

Also, one of the other rooms with bunk beds is completely empty. Thus, we decide
