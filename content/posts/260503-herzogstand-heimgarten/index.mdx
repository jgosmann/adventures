To enjoy the splendid weather this long weekend even more, I'm doing a hike
summitting the Herzogstand (1731m) and crossing the ridge line to the Heimgarten
(1791m). It is a classic tour for people living in the Munich area, but so far
I have never done it because it can be expected to be quite busy and doesn't
pose much of a challenge to me. So it will be a rather a relaxed tour for me.

<GpxTrack src="20260503.gpx" />

After being dropped off from a full bus, I walk up a broad gravel path.
Surprisingly, it isn't that busy. I suppose most are lazy and take the gondola
up. Along the way, I get a few nice views of the Walchensee.

Eventually, I can leave the broad path and take the steeper ski slope that
criss-crosses it. But the first minor highlight is an unmarked, thin trail which
no one else is on and only joins back with the broad gravel path after about
100m of elevation or so.

I go around a cauldron to reach the Herzogstandhaus, a cabin close to the
cable car. The place is indeed quite overrun now. Many people are only
wearing sneakers which is not necessarily the best choice of shoes for the
alpine environment.

Before joining the masses and heading to the Herzogstand summit, I take
a smaller trail up to the Martinskopf (1676m). Up here it is quieter and the
view is already good. Next I take another unmarked trail along the ridge line
leading back to the main trail after some fights with mountain pines.

On top of the Herzogstand, I have a lunch break to prepare myself for the next
and most difficult part of the tour: crossing the ridge line towards the
Heimgarten. The trail is marked with “black” difficulty, the highest rating for
hiking trails in the German Alps. Indeed it is a rocky trail, requiring the
hands in a few spots, sometimes a bit exposed, but in these spots always well
secured with steel cables. It is a bit less busy here, as the trail might not be
suitable for everyone without any alpine experience, but there are still some
people (and still some with sneakers).

Then I continue to follow the ridge line even farther and it is surprisingly
quiet again. At the Buchrain (1456m) the trail ends and I have to descend. The
signs say it is 1h 45min to Ohlstadt. But the next train departs already in 1h
10min, and then it is an hour wait for the next one. I know that I will be
faster than the posted time, but will I be that fast? Challenge accepted! So
I make my way down a nice and quiet trail. After no more than 30 minutes, I pass
a sign giving an estimate of 1h, so the total difference between remaining time
and estimate got less. This trend continues and eventually my remaining time is
more than the estimate on the signs. In the end, I comfortably reach the train
station with more than 10 minutes to spare. And then, of course, the train is
also delayed by a couple of minutes.

<Rimg

The hike had a length of 19km, 1200m of elevation gain, 1300m of elevation loss,
and took me 4h 15min without breaks. My average speed (without breaks) was
4.4km/h.
